## Daur Language

{% include icon.html language="dta" %}

### Daur written with Cyrillic in 1916 orthography

{% include icon.html script="Cyrl" language="dta" year="1916" %}

In 1916, Qin Tongfu (钦同甫) created this writing system using Cyrillic letters to write the Daur language. This writing system was once promoted in the Butha (布特哈) region and had some influence.

The only surviving record of this script is a photograph taken by the renowned photographer Siqin (思勤) at the Molidawa (莫力达瓦) Cultural Center; the original documents were later destroyed in a fire. The photograph was published in the _Daur Material Collection_. Based on this photograph, Enghebatu (恩和巴图) compiled an entry for the _Daur Encyclopedia Dictionary_, which subsequently became the source for all later descriptions, including Wu (2015) [^1] and Ding Shiqing’s _Outline of the Daur Language_. However, due to the poor quality of the photograph, several letters were misidentified in this chain of transmission.

Remark:

1. The relationship between letters and phonemes is entirely inferred, as no text samples with known readings survive. The assignments above are based on the letter positions in the original table and comparison with later Daur Cyrillic schemes (such as the 1956 orthography) and other Mongolic Cyrillic alphabets.
2. ⟨<span class="cyrl">є</span>⟩ (Ukrainian Ye) likely represents the front vowel <span class="ipa">/e/</span>.
3. ⟨<span class="cyrl">ѣ</span>⟩ (Yat) was misread as ⟨<span class="cyrl">е</span>⟩ or ⟨<span class="cyrl">ъ</span>⟩ in earlier descriptions due to the poor quality of the photograph. It probably represents <span class="ipa">/jə/</span>.
4. ⟨<span class="cyrl">і</span>⟩ (dotted I) may represent <span class="ipa">/jɔ/</span> or serve as a palatalizing element. It may also have been used for diphthongs such as <span class="ipa">/ai/</span> and <span class="ipa">/ei/</span>.
5. ⟨<span class="cyrl">ы</span>⟩ may have been used for diphthongs (<span class="ipa">/ai/</span>, <span class="ipa">/ei/</span>, <span class="ipa">/oi/</span>) or to transcribe apical vowels in Chinese loanwords, similar to its function in the 1956 orthography.
6. ⟨<span class="cyrl">ь</span>⟩ and ⟨<span class="cyrl">ъ</span>⟩ are modelled on the Russian situation: ⟨<span class="cyrl">ь</span>⟩ for palatalization, ⟨<span class="cyrl">ъ</span>⟩ as a hard sign indicating a hiatus. However, ⟨<span class="cyrl">ъ</span>⟩ may also have been used for reduced vowels, as in early Kalmyk Cyrillic.
7. ⟨<span class="cyrl">ѳ</span>⟩ (Fita) is of uncertain function; it may mark labialized consonants or serve another auxiliary purpose.
8. ⟨<span class="cyrl">в</span>⟩ is considered in some literature to record <span class="ipa">/t͡sʰ/</span>, which is here assigned to ⟨<span class="cyrl">ц</span>⟩. The absence of ⟨<span class="cyrl">з</span>⟩ and ⟨<span class="cyrl">щ</span>⟩ is expected, as Daur lacks <span class="ipa">/z/</span> and the sounds they typically represent.

### Daur written with Cyrillic in 1956 orthography

{% include icon.html script="Cyrl" language="dta" year="1956" %}

In May 1956, a scientific symposium on the Mongolian language was held in Hohhot city. The draft of the writing system was discussed at the meeting, and was sent out for extensive consultation and trial teaching in some areas, which received a favorable response; a working conference on the Daur language was held in Hohhot in December, and the writing system was adopted. The orthography was compiled by Oonan Ujur (欧南·乌珠尔) and published as _Daur Orthography_ (Draft) in April 1957. In the same year, Inner Mongolia established a specialized institution, published a series of textbooks and readers, and conducted trial teaching. However, the script promotion was halted in 1958. The writing system records the Daur phonology based on the Nawen subdialect (纳文土语), Butha dialect (布特哈方言) in Molidawa Daur autonomous banner (莫利达瓦达斡尔族自治旗), Hulunbuir city (呼伦贝尔市).

Remark:

1. ⟨<span class="cyrl">я</span>⟩, ⟨<span class="cyrl">е</span>⟩, ⟨<span class="cyrl">ё</span>⟩, ⟨<span class="cyrl">ю</span>⟩ are single letters representing the sequences of ⟨<span class="cyrl">й</span>⟩ + ⟨<span class="cyrl">а</span>⟩, ⟨<span class="cyrl">э</span>⟩, ⟨<span class="cyrl">о</span>⟩, ⟨<span class="cyrl">у</span>⟩.
2. ⟨<span class="cyrl">е</span>⟩ has two readings: after a consonant it represents the front vowel <span class="ipa">/je/</span>; elsewhere it represents <span class="ipa">/jə/</span>. When conditions do not permit ⟨<span class="cyrl">ё</span>⟩, ⟨<span class="cyrl">е</span>⟩ may be used instead, distinguished by meaning and vowel harmony.
3. ⟨<span class="cyrl">ы</span>⟩ has two readings: (1) a diphthong <span class="ipa">/əj/</span> (<span class="ipa">/aj/</span>, <span class="ipa">/ej/</span>, <span class="ipa">/oj/</span>) that does not appear word-initially; (2) an apical vowel <span class="ipa">/ɨ/</span> used in Chinese loanwords.
4. ⟨<span class="cyrl">ь</span>⟩ and ⟨<span class="cyrl">ъ</span>⟩ are similar to [Russian written with Cyrillic](https://kushim-jiang.github.io/scripts/2024-10-28-cyrl/#russian-written-with-cyrillic-in-1700-orthography): ⟨<span class="cyrl">ь</span>⟩ for palatalization, ⟨<span class="cyrl">ъ</span>⟩ as a hard sign.
